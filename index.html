Erosion & sea

erosion runs actual droplets over the grid: each one picks up sediment where the slope is steep and drops it where the slope flattens, which is what cuts the branching valleys a pure noise field never has. it is the slowest step — turn it down while you place nodes.

wire mesh
the rungs between one section and the next, hidden by the same horizon the rows use. square cells spaces them to match the rows on screen and ignores the slider.
plate shape
convergence narrows the far rows, depth ease crowds them toward the back, plate turn swings the section line around the map and sample zoom decides how much ground the plate covers.
strata
the ground under the front of the plate, drawn as beds. flattening takes the deep beds off the shape of the surface and down onto the floor; folding gives each one a warp of its own. shear the plate to swing a flank into view.

Flow

lines follow the downhill gradient, so at 0° they are drainage. rotate toward 90° and they turn to run across the slope instead — the same field, combed the other way.

Hachure

lehmann's rule: strokes point straight downhill and get heavier as the ground steepens, so the flats stay empty paper.

Rivers & paths

rivers come out of the flow accumulation — every cell's water sent to its lowest neighbour, the channels appearing where enough of it has collected. paths are least-cost routes between a shoreline and a summit, and the grade aversion is what makes them break into switchbacks instead of going straight up. plan views only.

Export

the map is a set of polylines, not an image, so the svg is real geometry — grouped by weight, ready to plot, cut or engrave. the json carries the parameters and every node, so a map you like comes back exactly.
